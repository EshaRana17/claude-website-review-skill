CTAs, benefit bullets and the honesty rules.

## 1. Voice profile

Before rewriting, read all copy the user supplied and write a short profile:

- **Tone**: pick two or three words (warm, plain, confident, playful, formal,
  technical, irreverent).
- **Person**: we/you, I/you, or third person.
- **Rhythm**: average sentence length; uses fragments or not.
- **Vocabulary**: words the brand repeats; words it avoids; local spelling
  (British or American English).
- **Formality markers**: contractions, emoji use (keep none if the brand uses none),
  exclamation marks.

Match these in every rewrite. If the existing voice is itself the problem (for
example vague corporate filler), say so and offer one option in the current voice
and one in a sharper voice, letting the owner choose.

## 2. Core message worksheet

Fill these from the page or by asking:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| Customer | Who exactly buys this? What do they call themselves? |
| Trigger | What happened that made them look today? |
| Problem | What is painful, slow, costly or risky without this? |
| Outcome | What is better after? Ideally observable. |
| Mechanism | How does this business deliver it differently? |
| Proof | What real evidence exists (reviews, numbers, clients, years)? |
| Objection | What is the biggest reason people hesitate? |

If the outcome or mechanism is unknown, do not guess wildly; offer two plausible
framings and ask which is true.

## 3. Headline angles

Write one option per angle, then recommend one. Aim for under about 10 words for the
main line; clarity beats cleverness.

| Angle | Pattern | Example (fictional dog groomer) |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | [Result] without [pain] | Calm, clean dogs without the all-day drop-off |
| Problem | Name the pain directly | Grooming visits that do not stress your dog out |
| Audience call-out | For [who] who [situation] | Mobile grooming for nervous and senior dogs |
| Differentiator | The [category] that [unique thing] | The grooming van that comes to your driveway |
| Specific | Concrete detail or number the user supplied | One dog at a time, 90 minutes, at your door |

Rationale lines should explain the trade-off, for example: "Best for search and ad
traffic because it names the category; less emotional than the outcome option."

Avoid: puns that hide the category, "Welcome to", "Your partner in", "Solutions",
"Next-generation", stacked adjectives, and questions that can be answered "no".

## 4. Subheadlines

The subheadline does the work the headline skipped. Pick the missing piece:

- **How**: "We bring a fully equipped van, so your dog never leaves home."
- **Who and where**: "Serving north Bristol, Tuesday to Saturday."
- **Proof** (only real): "Rated 4.9 by 212 customers on Google." Use only numbers the
  user gave; otherwise write `[rating] from [count] reviews on [platform]`.

Keep to one or two sentences.

## 5. CTA variants

A strong CTA is a verb, plus the thing they get, plus (optionally) low commitment.

| Weak | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Submit | Send my quote request | Says what happens |
| Learn more | See how it works | Specific destination |
| Sign up | Start my free trial | Ownership plus value |
| Contact us | Book a 15-minute call | Concrete, small commitment |
| Buy now | Add to basket, free returns | Risk reducer, only if true |

First person ("my") often reads naturally on buttons; test it if traffic allows,
otherwise pick whichever matches the voice.

Microcopy under the button answers "what happens next" or "what is the risk":
"We reply within one working day", "No card required", "Takes 2 minutes". Only use
lines the user confirms are true.

## 6. Feature to benefit bullets

Formula: **[Feature] so [customer outcome]**, or lead with the outcome and support it
with the feature.

| Feature (before) | Benefit-led (after) |
|---|---|
| Online booking system | Book a slot in under a minute, any time of day |
| Hypoallergenic shampoo | Gentle on sensitive skin, with products we can name for you |
| 10 years of experience | A groomer who has handled anxious dogs for a decade |

Rules:

- Lead each bullet with the most important word for the reader.
- Keep parallel structure and similar length.
- Three to six bullets per block.
- Keep the feature visible; benefit-only bullets can feel vague.

## 7. Proof and honesty rules

- Never invent testimonials, names, ratings, review counts, customer numbers,
  percentages, awards, press mentions, client logos, certifications or guarantees.
- Never imply endorsements the business does not have.
- Superlatives ("best", "number one", "fastest") need evidence; suggest softer,
  checkable alternatives.
- Placeholders must be obviously placeholders: `[customer quote: ask a recent client
  what changed]`.
- Comparative claims about competitors should be factual and verifiable.
- Pricing and guarantee lines must come from the user.
- Advertising rules vary by country and sector (health, finance, legal). If the copy
  touches regulated claims, recommend the owner checks local rules.

End each rewrite with a "Proof to collect" list that turns placeholders into tasks.

## 8. Editing pass

Before delivering, check each line:

- Could a competitor paste this onto their site unchanged? If yes, make it specific.
- Is there a word the customer would not use? Replace it with theirs.
- Read it aloud: does it sound like the brand?
- Does every section end with a reason or route to act?
- Reading level: aim for plain language most adults read easily; short sentences,
  common words.
